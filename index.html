The preparation, at a glance

Summary

Elliot Semmelman prepares Temple Beth David students to lead their B’nai Mitzvah service: reading and chanting prayers, inviting congregational English readings, chanting Torah and Haftarah, and delivering a d’var Torah about their portion’s relevance to their lives.

A personalized starting point

About 10 months before the date, Elliot checks Hebrew reading skills with the student’s teacher or evaluates the student himself. Formal training begins 6 months before the date, or 8 months before when extra support is needed. A family meeting sets out the plan, resources, and expectations.

Skills built in sequence

The first month reviews prayers and blessings. Torah practice progresses from fluent reading with vowels to trope melodies, then to Torah script without vowels or punctuation. Students then learn Haftarah with its different melody, using vowels and musical notes.

Support between sessions

A notebook includes the service, readings, and supporting material. A personalized online program provides a progress timeline, Hebrew texts in different formats, and audio recordings. Custom digital tutorials can address individual needs.

Confidence through rehearsal

Sessions usually take place on Zoom, with in-person meetings available. Near the date, students rehearse on the sanctuary bimah with a microphone, practicing the service, pacing, page-turn pauses, and connection with the congregation.

Practice rhythm: Start with about 30 minutes per week between sessions. Around 2 months before the date, increase to 10–15 minutes daily, adjusted to the student’s needs.

A step-by-step preparation plan

Outline
  1. Define the goal: lead the service
    • Read and chant prayers; invite the congregation to read English sections.
    • Chant Torah and Haftarah and deliver a d’var Torah connecting the portion to the student’s life.
  2. Assess Hebrew readiness · about 10 months before
    • Consult the Hebrew teacher or evaluate the student directly.
    • Begin training 6 months before the date for a capable reader, or 8 months before if extra help is needed.
  3. Meet with the student and family
    • Explain the path ahead, teaching resources, and student expectations.
    • Plan weekly 30-minute sessions, typically on Zoom; in-person sessions and scheduling adjustments are possible.
  4. Review prayers and blessings · first month
    • Check reading and chanting skills for prayers previously learned in Jewish school.
    • Coach English readings and difficult words.
  5. Master Torah readings
    • The source describes Beth David’s minimum as “3 verses from 3 aliyot.”
    • Read a verse fluently with vowels, then learn its trope symbols, phrases, and melodies.
    • Practice from copies of Torah script, without vowels or punctuation, until ready.
    • Repeat for all assigned verses and aliyot.
  6. Learn Haftarah
    • Students typically recite 3 verses from the Prophets related to the week’s Torah portion.
    • Learn a different melody; vowels and musical notes remain available.
  7. Practice with ongoing support
    • Use the notebook, personalized online timeline, texts, and audio.
    • Add custom tutorials for specific needs, such as distinguishing similar Hebrew letters.
    • Practice about 30 minutes weekly initially, increasing to 10–15 minutes daily around 2 months before the date.
  8. Rehearse in the sanctuary
    • Run through the service and readings on the bimah with the microphone on.
    • Refine pacing, pauses for congregation page turns, and audience contact.
